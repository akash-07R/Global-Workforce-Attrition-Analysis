# Business Insights

## Dataset Overview

The dataset contains 1,470 employee records and 35 original fields.

- Total Employees: 1,470
- Employees with Attrition: 237
- Overall Attrition Rate: 16.1%

The following observations describe patterns present in the dataset.
They should be interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships.


## 1. Overtime and Attrition

Employees recorded as working overtime had an observed attrition rate of
30.5%, compared with 10.4% among employees without overtime.

- Overtime: 416 employees, 127 attritions
- No Overtime: 1,054 employees, 110 attritions

This represents one of the largest differences in observed attrition rates
among the analyzed workforce segments.



## 2. Attrition by Job Role

The observed attrition rate varied substantially across job roles.

The highest observed rates were:

- Sales Representative: 39.76%
- Laboratory Technician: 23.94%
- Human Resources: 23.08%
- Sales Executive: 17.48%

The lowest observed rates were:

- Research Director: 2.5%
- Manager: 4.9%
- Manufacturing Director: 6.9%
- Healthcare Representative: 6.9%



## 3. Age Group

The 18–25 age group had an observed attrition rate of 35.8%.

Other age groups:

- 26–35: 19.1%
- 36–45: 9.2%
- 46–55: 11.5%
- 56+: 17.0%

The dataset therefore shows substantial variation in attrition across
age groups.



## 4. Company Tenure

Employees in the New tenure band (0–2 years at the company) had an observed
attrition rate of 29.8%.

The rates for other tenure groups were:

- Early Career: 13.8%
- Established: 12.3%
- Experienced: 6.5%
- Long Tenure: 9.4%

This indicates that attrition is more concentrated among employees with
shorter company tenure in this dataset.



## 5. Salary Band

The observed attrition rate varied across salary bands:

- Low: 28.6%
- Lower-Mid: 12.7%
- Mid: 12.0%
- Upper-Mid: 13.5%
- High: 3.8%

The dashboard can therefore be used to examine the relationship between
compensation segments and observed attrition patterns.



## 6. Business Travel

Observed attrition rates by business travel category were:

- Travel Frequently: 24.9%
- Travel Rarely: 15.0%
- Non-Travel: 8.0%

Employees in the frequent-travel category showed a higher observed
attrition rate than the other travel categories.



## 7. Employee Satisfaction

Job satisfaction also showed variation in observed attrition:

- Low satisfaction: 22.8%
- Medium satisfaction: 16.4%
- High satisfaction: 16.5%
- Very High satisfaction: 11.3%

Environment satisfaction showed a similar pattern, with the lowest
satisfaction category having a 25.4% observed attrition rate.



## 8. Compensation Analysis

Average monthly income increased substantially across job levels:

- Level 1: 2,787
- Level 2: 5,502
- Level 3: 9,817
- Level 4: 15,504
- Level 5: 19,192

Average monthly income also varied considerably by job role, with Manager
and Research Director roles having the highest average monthly income in
this dataset.



## 9. Performance Rating

The dataset contains two performance-rating categories:

- Rating 3: 1,244 employees
- Rating 4: 226 employees

Observed attrition rates were relatively similar:

- Rating 3: 16.1%
- Rating 4: 16.4%

Therefore, performance rating alone does not show a large difference in
observed attrition within this dataset.



## Analytical Note

These findings describe patterns in the available dataset and should not
be interpreted as evidence that overtime, salary, age, tenure, satisfaction,
or other variables directly cause employee attrition.

Additional organizational data and statistical analysis would be required
to investigate causal relationships.
